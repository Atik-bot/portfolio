Time-Series · Graph ML · Undergraduate Thesis 2025

Stock Price Prediction Using a Hybrid GCN–RNN Deep Learning Framework

Stock prices are volatile and never move in isolation, yet time-series models (LSTM/RNN) ignore cross-stock dependencies while graph models (GCN) miss temporal dynamics. This thesis proposes a unified spatiotemporal framework that captures both at once.

Objective
Jointly model inter-stock relationships and time-based price patterns in a single architecture, and beat standalone baselines on real market data.
Approach
OHLCV data for technology equities (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA) from Yahoo Finance — cleaned, normalized, and windowed into sequences with an 80/20 split. Historical prices are converted into a correlation-based graph (stocks as nodes, correlation strength as edges); the GCN learns spatial dependencies and its output feeds an RNN for sequential learning.
Models
Proposed GCN+RNN hybrid, benchmarked against GCN, RNN, LSTM, LSTM+RNN, and GCN+LSTM.
Results
The hybrid achieved the best overall performance — R² = 0.8941 and MSE = 0.0037 — with the highest R² and lowest MAE among all six models, confirming that joint spatiotemporal learning improves financial forecasting.

HCI · AR · Sequence Modeling 2025 – 2026

Predictive Intent Modeling for In-Air Micro-Gestures to Mitigate the Midas Touch Problem in AR Interfaces

AR gesture recognition is reactive — gestures are classified only after they complete, causing latency and the "Midas Touch" effect where stray hand movements trigger unintended commands. This work predicts user intent mid-gesture instead.

Objective
Recognize gesture intent before the gesture finishes, cutting response latency and false activations while keeping interaction natural and low-fatigue.
Approach
SHREC'21 skeleton dataset (180 sequences, 17 gesture classes). Each frame's 140-D hand skeleton is expanded into 327-D kinematic features — per-joint velocity and acceleration, fingertip distances, trajectory smoothness, and hand stability — processed through a fully causal pipeline. A confidence-aware decision layer then issues a three-way verdict per prediction: FIRE (≥0.80), WAIT (0.50–0.80), or REJECT (<0.50).
Models
Proposed causal dilated TCN, benchmarked against a bidirectional LSTM baseline and a Transformer encoder under identical training.
Results
The TCN leads with 92.94% accuracy and F1, 0.52 ms inference (2.5× faster than LSTM), and the lowest false-positive rate (0.44%). It reaches 88.5% accuracy at just 60% gesture completion — responding ~40% earlier than reactive systems — and FIRE-state decisions are 99.5% accurate, effectively neutralizing Midas Touch activations.

Marketing Analytics · Classification 2026

Enhancing Social Media Advertising through Machine Learning Techniques

Predicting whether a digital ad campaign will convert is hard to model with conventional statistics, since engagement depends on a tangle of cost, audience, and behavioral signals. This work benchmarks six classifiers to find which approach handles that complexity best.

Objective
Predict customer conversion from digital marketing campaign data and identify which class of model — linear, tree-based, or ensemble — handles real-world engagement patterns most reliably.
Approach
8,000-customer Kaggle digital marketing dataset (20 variables: demographics, spend, click-through and conversion rates, site visits, loyalty). Identifier and constant columns were dropped, categorical fields label-encoded, and all features Z-score normalized. Conversion is imbalanced (87.6% / 12.4%), so the decision threshold for high-engagement campaigns was lowered to 0.4 to favor recall, and models were scored on a 25% hold-out set.
Models
Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbors, Naive Bayes, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting, compared on accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and ROC-AUC.
Results
Gradient Boosting led every metric that mattered — F1 = 0.9556, recall = 0.9943, and the highest ROC-AUC at 0.826 — with Random Forest close behind (F1 = 0.9460, AUC = 0.817). Logistic Regression struggled to generalize (71.4% accuracy) and KNN had the weakest discrimination (AUC = 0.655), confirming that ensemble tree methods capture the non-linear structure of ad engagement far better than linear baselines.
